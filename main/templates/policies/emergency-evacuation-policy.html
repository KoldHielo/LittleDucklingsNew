I plan ahead for emergencies so that I can stay calm and keep every child safe. This includes fire, a gas leak, flood, loss of water or power, damage to the building, lockdown or any other reason the home becomes unsafe.

Preparation

  • Working smoke detection and appropriate fire-control equipment are maintained and tested. Escape routes and identifiable exits are checked daily, kept clear and can be opened easily from inside.
  • The primary and alternative routes, outdoor assembly point and temporary safe location are recorded in the setting's premises plan and explained to families. Plans do not publish security-sensitive detail online.
  • An evacuation aid is available for babies and non-mobile children. My charged phone, attendance information, emergency contacts and essential medication are kept ready to take where doing so does not delay escape.
  • Emergency contacts, individual evacuation needs and reasonable adjustments are reviewed with families.

Evacuation

I will raise the alarm, call 999 when safe, gather children calmly, take the safest route and never delay for belongings. Babies and non-mobile children will be moved using the planned evacuation method. At the assembly point I will take the register and complete a headcount. Nobody will re-enter until the emergency service says it is safe. Families will be contacted and children cared for at the temporary safe location until safely collected.

Other emergencies and lockdown

Where leaving would increase risk, I will secure the children in the safest available area, maintain supervision, call emergency services and follow their instructions. Loss of utilities or unsafe premises will result in closure or relocation where safe care cannot continue.

Practice and review

I practise different escape routes and record each drill in ChildLogs, including who was present, how long it took and anything that needs to improve. After a drill or real emergency, I update the plan and tell families about any important changes.
