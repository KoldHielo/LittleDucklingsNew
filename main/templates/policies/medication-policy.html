I administer medicine only when it is necessary, within my competence and in the child's best interests. No prescription or non-prescription medicine is given without written permission for that particular medicine. A text or email may provide written evidence; verbal-only consent is never sufficient.

Before administration

  • Prescription medicine must have been prescribed for that child. Aspirin is given only when prescribed by a doctor.
  • Parents provide the original labelled container with the child's name, medicine, strength, dose, route, frequency, expiry, storage instructions and reason. I check that instructions and consent agree and clarify any discrepancy before giving it.
  • Long-term or emergency medicine is supported by an individual healthcare/allergy plan and appropriate training. I may decline administration I cannot perform safely and will agree an alternative plan with the family and clinician.

Storage and administration

Medicine is inaccessible to children, in original packaging and stored according to its instructions; refrigeration is used only where required, in a secure labelled container. Emergency medicine remains readily accessible to the responsible adult. Expiry dates are checked and unused or expired medicine returned to the parent for safe disposal.

I check the right child, medicine, dose, route and time, and use clean equipment. Each administration is recorded immediately: child, medicine, dose, route, date/time, reason, administrator, and any refusal, error or reaction. Parents are told the same day or as soon as reasonably practicable and acknowledge the record.

Errors, refusal or reaction

I will not force a medicine. I will follow the healthcare plan and product advice, contact the parent and seek clinical help from NHS 111 or 999 as appropriate. Errors and adverse reactions are recorded, disclosed promptly, risk-assessed and reported externally where required.
