The child's welfare is paramount. Laura Oldfield is the safeguarding lead and acts immediately where a child may need help or protection, following the EYFS statutory framework for childminders, Working Together to Safeguard Children 2026, Prevent duty guidance and Stoke-on-Trent Safeguarding Children Partnership procedures.

Recognising harm

I remain alert to abuse, harm and neglect, including physical, emotional and sexual abuse; neglect; domestic abuse and coercive control; child-on-child harm; online harm; criminal and sexual exploitation; trafficking; radicalisation; fabricated or induced illness; forced marriage; honour- or faith-based abuse; and female genital mutilation (FGM). Disabled children, babies, children with communication needs, children in care and children affected by discrimination may face additional barriers to being heard.

Signs can include a disclosure, unexplained or unusual injury, change in behaviour or wellbeing, fear, sexualised behaviour, hunger or poor hygiene, concerning adult behaviour, repeated/unexplained absence, online indicators or a pattern that does not fit the child's development. I use professional curiosity without stereotyping.

Responding to a concern or disclosure

  • I listen calmly, take the child seriously, do not promise secrecy, ask only open questions needed to clarify, and explain what will happen in language the child understands.
  • I make a timely factual record using the child's own words where possible, with date, time, people present, observations and action. I do not ask the child to repeat an account unnecessarily.
  • I refer child-safety or welfare concerns immediately to Stoke-on-Trent Integrated Front Door under local procedures. I call 999 where there is immediate danger or urgent crime, and 101 for non-emergency police advice.
  • I normally work openly with parents, but will not inform them first where that may increase risk, prejudice an investigation or conflict with agency advice. I do not investigate, confront an alleged perpetrator or interview witnesses before referral.

Local contacts: Integrated Front Door 01782 235100 (Monday–Thursday 8:30am–5pm; Friday 8:30am–4:30pm). Emergency Duty Team 01782 234234 outside those hours. Police 999 in an emergency or 101 otherwise.

Allegations about adults and suitability

Any allegation of harm or abuse by anyone living, working or looking after children at the premises is referred without delay to Children's Social Care/LADO through the Integrated Front Door, wherever the alleged event occurred. Protective action is agreed with the proper authorities. I notify Ofsted of the allegation and action taken as soon as reasonably practicable and always within 14 days.

Household members and any assistant, volunteer, student or contractor are managed under current suitability requirements. Required enhanced criminal-record checks and references are completed; a new employee does not start before the required check is received. Nobody who is unchecked or not cleared by Ofsted may have unsupervised contact with children. Relevant changes affecting suitability or people aged 16 or over living/working on the premises are notified as required.

Training and safe practice

  • Safeguarding-lead training covers the criteria in EYFS Annex C, is renewed at least every two years, and knowledge is kept current between courses, including local referral routes, online safety and emerging harms.
  • Visitors are recorded and supervised. Professional boundaries, intimate care, safer recruitment, allegations, whistleblowing and lone-working risks are applied consistently.
  • Attendance is monitored under the Attendance Policy. Unexpected or prolonged absence leads to contact attempts and safeguarding escalation based on risk, not an arbitrary waiting period.
  • Collections use written authorisation and identity checks. Failure to collect is managed under the Uncollected Child Policy.
  • Images are taken only for an agreed childcare purpose with appropriate written permission and are stored/shared securely. Personal devices and smart wearables are not used to photograph children. Emergency communication and approved learning use are permitted without weakening supervision.
  • I have regard to DfE screen-use guidance. Online content is age appropriate, filtered or supervised, and does not displace responsive care, play, movement or sleep.

Specific safeguarding matters

FGM: FGM is illegal and a form of child abuse. I refer any risk, suspicion or disclosure immediately through safeguarding procedures and to police where urgent. The specific mandatory police-reporting duty for teachers and regulated health/social-care professionals is not inaccurately claimed to apply solely because I am a childminder.

Prevent: I notice signs that a child or family may be vulnerable to radicalisation, record and refer concerns through local safeguarding/Prevent routes, and call 999 for immediate danger. I build resilience through belonging, respectful discussion and age-appropriate critical thinking without stigmatising a faith, culture or community.

Food and allergy safety: Individual allergy action plans, texture/weaning information, cross-contamination controls, choking-safe preparation, sight-and-hearing supervision and an appropriately qualified paediatric first-aider in the room are maintained as set out in the Health and Safety Policy.

Information sharing, records and escalation

I share relevant, accurate, necessary and proportionate information promptly where this protects a child; consent is not required where seeking it would place a child at risk or frustrate protection. Safeguarding records are secured, separated where appropriate and retained under the data policy. If I remain concerned after a referral, I use the local escalation process. Anyone concerned about how safeguarding is handled may use the Whistleblowing Policy.
